Silvia Wiring Reference

Every run in the custom harness, with its ID, ends, wire and starting length. The IDs match the printed wire labels. Tap a wire, a tag or a row to see that run on its own, tap a part to see every run that lands on it, or step through the runs with the arrows.

Cross-check, not a validated schematic

There is no official Silvia schematic. This is redrawn from realNiche's community diagram (PCB v3.1/v4.0) with lengths from Talon's harness and the older RS diagram. Check each run against realNiche's original before you crimp, and pass the power-on checks before the machine is plugged in.

The Shelly runs (SH1 to SH4) and the neutral splice aren't on realNiche's diagram, and nothing about the Shelly is in the official docs. They start from gblur's Silvia V6 E drawing from his build video, which shows a Shelly 1 with the inlet's line on both its L and I terminals and his neutral straight from the inlet. This site draws the 1PM, which joins L to its relay inside, on its own leads from the inlet, so the machine's neutral never runs through it and nothing uses its terminals as a bridge, which Shelly's docs warn against. The two-wire inlet spades and the neutral splice are this site's own. Runs tagged changed for the Shelly differ from realNiche's diagram. gblur's drawing shows a 230 V solenoid, so it's likely of a 230 V machine; this site uses the same runs at 120 V too. The Shelly guide covers the part, where it goes and how to set it up.

Spades and routing

Brew, steam, hot water

Use one pole of each switch, the ground pole. The PCB signal goes on its center spade and ground on its outer spade. The other pole stays free; the LED mod puts 5 V on its outer spade.

Power switch

It stays on mains and breaks both wires: line through one pole, neutral through the other. Supply on the outer spades, loads on the center spades. Its own lamp keeps working.

Power switch, with the ShellyShelly

It only tells the Shelly to switch, but it still carries mains: AC1 brings line to the L pole's outer spade and SH2 takes it from the center spade to the Shelly's SW. The N pole stays empty. If the switch still broke neutral, the app couldn't turn the machine on with the switch off, so AC2 meets AC8 and AC9 in a splice instead and the Shelly's relay breaks line only. A lamp inside the switch, or a power light fed from it, may stay dark now; gblur's drawing has a power LED on the screen's 5 V line instead.

At the boiler

Uninsulated spades crimped with an open-barrel crimper, then wrapped in self-fusing silicone tape. Nylon-insulated spades are rated to 105 °C and stay away from the boiler. The solenoid sits on the group and gets hot too, so bare spades and tape are the safer choice there as well.

Doubled spades

Two 18 AWG wires never go into a red spade or fork. Use a blue one, or a bare spade sized for 16 to 14 AWG at the boiler, wherever two runs share a terminal: SSR terminal 1, the safety thermostat, the power switch's neutral spade, the inlet's L and N tabs, the lamp, the pump, the solenoid and the boiler ground. The neutral splice is a BN2 butt splice: AC2 on one side, AC8 and AC9 on the other.

Keep mains and low voltage apart

Route mains high and low voltage low, with the PCB connections to the right. Only 200 °C silicone wire may touch the boiler or steam wand. Talon ran mains, the pump hose and the pump wiring through the center slot at the back.

Earth and 5 V ground

Earth goes to the boiler ground spade and the solenoid's ground spade. It never connects to the PCB's 5 V ground, which floats so the thermocouple reads correctly.

Heating light from V5 onV5 and V6

The vertical bar lamp became a round LED beside the power switch. Trace where its wires go before you cut: stock, it usually runs from the switched line to the element terminal it shares with the steam thermostat. The new harness puts it across the element instead, AC7 from the heater line after the safety thermostat and AC8 from neutral, so it lights while the SSR heats. If it has more than two wires, or the E's energy-saver module drives it, ask on Discord before rewiring it.

E model power switchE model

The E's power switch is a push button that works with its GICAR energy-saver module. This harness leaves the module out and needs a power switch that latches, breaking line and neutral like the M's double-pole rocker. Fit one before wiring AC1, AC2, AC3, AC8 and AC9.

E model power button, with the ShellyE model

The GICAR energy-saver module still comes out, but the push button can stay: it feeds the Shelly's SW input, set to Button, so each press turns the machine on or off. That's this site's suggestion; gblur's drawing doesn't show which switch he kept. The Shelly's Auto off can turn it off a set time after it comes on, whether it's in use or not. The button now carries mains, so keep it only if the rating on its body says 250 V. If it doesn't, fit a latching double-pole rocker like the M's and set the Shelly to Switch.

230 V machines230 V

realNiche's diagram covers 110 to 240 V and the harness is the same. The element reads about three times the resistance of a 120 V one (about 48 Ω against 15 Ω), and the protection on the circuit is an RCD rather than a GFCI.

Boilers before May 2000V1 early

The boiler changed design in May 2000. Confirm the thermocouple mounting and the terminal layout on yours before following the boiler end of this harness.
