The stock Silvia

A brass boiler, a vibratory pump and a thermostat that switches the heater fully on and fully off. The temperature swings above and below the target, which is why Silvia owners learned to time their shots by the heating light.

How the Silvia works
02

Plan and buy

There's no Silvia kit. The PCB and screen come from an approved supplier and the rest from the official parts list, plus a few Silvia parts: an M3 thermocouple, a pressure tee and extra wire.

03

Open it up

Cord out of the wall, top off. Photograph and label every stock wire before it comes out. The brew thermostat comes off the boiler; the safety thermostat stays.

RunbookWhich Silvia
04

Fit the hardware

The thermocouple screws into the boiler top. The SSR goes on the left wall, the PCB beside the valve, the pressure sensor on its tee, and the screen on top.

Runbook, phase 3
05

Wire the harness

Sixteen mains runs and eleven low-voltage ones, each with an ID and a label at both ends. Line runs through the SSR to the element, neutral chains back, earth goes to the boiler, and the switches move to low voltage.

Check it, unplugged

%%NCHECKS%% checks with the cord out: earth reaches the metal, nothing shorts, every path is whole, every switch input is clean. The checklist only says ready when all of them pass.

Power-on checks
07

First power-on

Covers on, plugged into a protected outlet. Steam knob open and flush until water runs from the wand, so the boiler is full before it heats. This time the temperature climbs and holds flat at the target.

08

Pull a profiled shot

Calibrate the pump, then let a profile run: a gentle preinfusion, a ramp to nine bar, and a taper as the puck gives way. The graph draws as the glass fills.
