Cord out, boiler cold

Unplug the machine and let the boiler cool before you open it. The drawings show what your hands do, not to scale. There's no official Silvia guide: these steps follow realNiche's harness and the Silvia builds on the Gaggiuino Discord, as the rest of this site does.

Thermocouple

K type · M3 · ungrounded

You need
  • The thermocouple: K type, ungrounded, M3 thread (not the official parts list's M4)
  • Thermal paste rated for 180 °C or more
  • A screwdriver for the thermostat's clamp screws, and a small flat one for J4
  • A multimeter
What it does

It reports the boiler's temperature to the PCB on J4, in place of the brew thermostat. It must be ungrounded (its junction insulated from the probe body) because the PCB's low-voltage side floats; How Gaggiuino works explains why.

    Pressure sensor

    1.2 MPa · OPV tee

    You need
    • The sensor (0 to 1.2 MPa, G1/4) and its flat white O-ring, about 11 mm across
    • Juun's parts: a 1/8" FFM tee and a 1/4"F to 1/8"M elbow
    • Loctite 55 thread cord
    • Two spanners
    • A little foam, if its readings turn out noisy
    What it does

    It tells the PCB the brew pressure, 0 to 12 bar, on J6, so Gaggiuino can profile pressure. It seals on its O-ring, not on its thread. No tap block fits a Silvia, so it goes on a tee.

      Making the wires

      cut · strip · crimp · tape · pull-test

      You need
      • 18 AWG 200 °C silicone wire for mains and 22 AWG for low voltage, cut from the cut list (a Silvia harness runs longer than a Gaggia one)
      • A wire stripper
      • An insulated crimper (red and blue dies) for spades, forks and butt splices
      • An open-barrel crimper for the bare boiler spades
      • Red and blue 6.3 mm spades, forks for the SSR (a blue one for the two wires on load terminal 1), uninsulated 6.3 mm spades, some sized for two wires (16 to 14 AWG), and red butt splices
      • Self-fusing silicone tape, an oil-based marker and your printed wire labels
      Which crimper is which

      An insulated crimper has colored, rounded dies and squashes a nylon sleeve. An open-barrel crimper has small B-shaped dies that roll a bare terminal's tabs over the wire. Most hardware-store crimpers are the insulated kind; the bare spades at the boiler need the open-barrel one.

        Solid-state relay

        40 A · left wall

        You need
        • The SSR: 3 to 32 V DC control, 24 to 480 V AC load, 40 A
        • One screw, washers and a nut, and a drill, or a small bracket
        • Forks for all four screws: one sized for two wires on load terminal 1
        • A screwdriver
        What it does

        It switches the element on the board's command, with the board's 5 V side isolated from mains. How Gaggiuino works explains it.

          Switches

          brew · steam · hot water · power

          You need
          • The low-voltage switch wires (22 AWG) with red spades, and red butt splices if you join the ground chain that way
          • The power switch's runs: AC1, AC2, AC3, AC8 and AC9, with one blue spade for AC8 and AC9
          • A latching double-pole rocker, in place of the E's push button
          What changes

          Each switch is two switches side by side (two poles) that flip together, each with a center and an outer spade. Brew, steam and hot water stop switching mains: one pole of each, when on, joins a PCB input to the 5 V ground. The power switch keeps mains and breaks both line and neutral.

            The screen, accessories and firmware

            Not wiring, but part of the build. The official guides are written for Gaggias, so their photos show a Gaggia; the Silvia notes come from the Discord thread.

            Screen housing

            Gen 3, 4.3"

            You need
            • The screen, and a cable long enough for your mount (see step 1)
            • The printed housing (back, front cover) and a mount
            • Heat-shrink for the vent, and Kapton or other high-temperature tape
            • For a front mount: steel for the magnets to hold to, or 80 °C double-sided tape
            What it does

            It holds the screen where you can reach it and keeps its cable away from sharp edges and mains wiring. The housing is the official one; the mount is the part that differs on a Silvia.

              ToFnLED

              optional · tank level

              You need
              • The ToFnLED board (or a standalone ToF board that runs on 3.3 V)
              • A Qwiic cable (JST-SH, 4-pin), 250 mm or more
              • A housing, its magnets and something steel for them to hold to (or 80 °C double-sided tape)
              What it does

              A time-of-flight sensor measures the distance down to the water, so the screen shows the tank level. It talks to the PCB over I2C on J2.

                Firmware updates

                Gen 3 · PCB v4

                PCB v3.x board repair. Some PCB v3.1, v3 and v2 boards can switch the heater on while a debugger is connected. The official PCB upgrade page has a test and a resistor fix; without it, flash those boards with the machine unplugged. PCB v4 doesn't need it. PA15 pull-down rework

                You need
                • A phone or computer on the machine's Wi-Fi
                • The three files from the official release
                • A screen or headless board enabled for Gen 3 by an official supplier, as the official flashing page requires
                • A computer with STM32CubeProgrammer
                • A WeAct Mini Debugger or an ST-Link, with four jumper wires
                What it does

                Updates the core (the STM32 on the PCB) and the screen (its ESP32 and the web UI). On PCB v4 all three go over Wi-Fi; the wire is the fallback for the core, and the only way on PCB v3.1.

                  Hardware scales

                  optional · weight in the cup

                  You need
                  • A Silvia load cell mount (Joscha's "beefy" remix) and two 1 kg load cells, 13 × 13 × 80 mm
                  • Four M4 × 10 mm countersunk screws, M5 × 10 mm low-profile screws (as many as the mount takes), four 5 × 2 mm magnets, gap-filling super glue
                  • Two HX711 boards and a 1 kΩ resistor, or a dual-scales board, and an enclosure
                  • Stripboard, five right-angle header pins, 26 AWG wire, a 5-pin JST-XH and a 5-pin JST-PH
                  • A 200 to 400 g object and an accurate scale, for calibration
                  What it does

                  Weighs the shot as it runs, through J8. Without them Gaggiuino estimates the weight (predictive scales), which is enough for most.
