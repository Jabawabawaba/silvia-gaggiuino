A vibratory pump can't be slowed down like a motor: every stroke is a fixed squirt. So the PCB controls how many strokes happen. A zero-cross detector tells the STM32 each time the mains wave passes zero, and for every cycle it decides whether to fire the triac that feeds the pump. Asking for 50% lets every other cycle through, 25% one in four, spread as evenly as possible. This is pulse skip modulation, PSM.

Pressure profiling closes the loop on the pressure sensor: the PCB reads it many times a second and raises or lowers the share of cycles to follow the profile's curve. Flow profiling works the other way round. Gaggiuino carries a model of the Ulka pump that says how much water one stroke moves at a given pressure, so it can turn a target flow into strokes per second, and it estimates the flow during every shot the same way. Pump Zero is that model's flow per stroke at 0 bar; the [UT] PZ Cal profile calibrates it against your scale, since no two pumps are quite alike.

The OPV still sits above all of this. Whatever a profile asks for, the pressure can't pass the OPV's setting, which is why Gaggiuino wants it at least 1 bar above your highest target.
