Hardware scales

under the drip tray, to J8

Two 1 kg load cells on a printed mount under the drip tray area weigh the cup as the shot runs, so Gaggiuino can stop at a target weight and plot it live. Without them it still predicts the weight from the pump's flow; a Bluetooth scale is the other option.

How it works

A load cell is a metal bar with strain gauges glued to it, wired as a bridge. The weight of the cup bends the bar by a tiny amount and the bridge puts out a few millivolts at most. Each cell has its own HX711 channel, which amplifies and measures that very finely; the two share the clock line on J8 and each has its own data line, D1 and D2. The PCB adds the two, subtracts the tare and converts to grams after you calibrate with a known weight.

Gaggiuino's parts list has 750 g cells. On a Silvia the mount of choice is Joscha's "beefy" remix, Printables 1277828, built for 13 × 13 × 80 mm 1 kg cells.

Tank level sensor

above the tank, to J2

The ToFnLED board's distance sensor, mounted above the water, tells the screen how full the tank is, so you see it running low before the pump runs dry. It plugs into the Qwiic socket, J2, on the back of the PCB. The board also has an RGB LED to light the tank; the Silvia's tank is covered, so builders use it for the level reading.

How it works

A time-of-flight sensor fires a very short pulse of infrared light and times how long the reflection takes to come back from the water surface. Light covers a millimeter in about 3 trillionths of a second, so the chip, a VL53L0X in the Gen 2 code, has to time it extremely finely; it reports a distance, and the firmware turns the distance into a percentage using the ranges you set. The Silvia's tank is deeper than a Gaggia's, about 200 mm, so those ranges need changing in the ToFnLED settings.

Switch lights

inside the three rockers

Once the brew, hot water and steam switches are on low voltage, their mains lamps go dark. The LED mod replaces each lamp with a small orange LED and resistor powered from the 5 V screen line, wired across the switch's spare pole so the light follows the switch. Shelly smart relay

an unofficial add-on, outside the case

A Shelly 1PM, a small Wi-Fi relay, sits between the inlet and the machine and switches it on and off from your phone or a schedule. The power switch stays on the front, but it now only tells the Shelly what to do. Nothing about it is in the official docs. This site's wiring starts from gblur's Silvia V6 E drawing, which shows a Shelly 1; it draws the 1PM instead.

How it works

The Shelly powers itself from L and N. Its relay connects L to O, and O feeds what the power switch's line pole used to, so with the relay open the PCB, the SSR and the heating lamp get nothing. Neutral no longer goes through the power switch: the relay breaks line only, where the stock switch broke both. Its SW input sees mains while the power switch is on: in Switch mode, with output type Toggle Switch, the relay follows the M's rocker; in Button mode each press of the E's push button toggles it; and the app can switch it either way. It also measures the power the machine draws. The PCB doesn't know it's there; it simply powers up when the relay closes.

The Shelly guide covers the part, where it goes and how to set it up.
