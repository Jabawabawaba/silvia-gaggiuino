The machine, mode by mode

side view, x-ray

Cord in, power off. Nothing has power, and the boiler is sealed: the 3-way valve and the steam knob are shut, and the pump only lets water through one way. Power on. The element heats the boiler until the brew thermostat opens, then waits for it to cool and close again, and the heating light follows. The boiler stays sealed, so the water heats where it is. Brew switch on. The coil lifts the 3-way valve's plunger: drain sealed, boiler open to the group. The pump pushes water into the boiler and hot water out onto the puck. Once the puck resists, the OPV sends the excess back to the tank, and coffee starts. Brew switch off. The pump stops and the spring drops the plunger: boiler port shut, group open to the drain. The pressure left above the puck rushes into the drip tray, and the boiler side stays sealed. Hot water switch on, steam knob open. The pump runs but the 3-way valve stays shut, so the only way out is the wand: fresh water in at the side, hot water out of the top. Just after steaming it sputters steam until the boiler is full. Steam switch on. It bypasses the brew thermostat, so the element heats on to the steam thermostat, well above boiling, and the light goes out. Then the knob: water spits out first, then steam from the top of the boiler. The level drops and the element comes back on.

The circuit

switch it yourself

Every load in the stock Silvia sits on its own path from line to neutral, and every path runs through a switch or a thermostat. Nothing is computed: if a path is closed end to end, that part runs. The diagram is drawn as a ladder, with line down the left, neutral down the right, and one rung per load.

Two details surprise people. The thermostats sit on the element's return side, after it, with only the safety thermostat ahead of it. And the heating light is wired from line to the point after the element, around the safety, so it shows the thermostats calling for heat even when a tripped safety has stopped the element.

Try it

  • Turn on Power and let it run: the element heats until the brew thermostat opens, then cycles.
  • Turn on Brew: the pump and the 3-way valve both run, from the brew switch's two poles.
  • Turn on Hot water instead: the pump runs, the valve doesn't.
  • Turn on Steam: it bridges the brew thermostat, so heating carries on until the steam thermostat opens at %%STEAM_OPEN%% °C.
  • Drag the temperature past 165 °C: the safety thermostat opens and stays open until it's reset. Drag it back down and the heating light comes on while the element stays cold.

With Gaggiuino, the brew, hot water and steam switches leave these rungs entirely and become low-voltage inputs to the PCB, and both regulating thermostats leave the element's circuit: the thermocouple and the SSR take over. The power switch, the safety thermostat, any pump thermal fuse and the earth stay as they are. See what changes.

A stock shot, start to finish

  1. Switch on. The element heats with the heating light on, and the boiler slowly warms the brass group bolted under it.
  2. The brew thermostat opens near its setting and the light goes out, but heat already in the element keeps the water rising for a while: overshoot.
  3. It cools several degrees, the thermostat closes, and the cycle repeats for as long as the machine is on. The water temperature at any moment depends on where you are in that cycle.
  4. Owners temperature-surf: flush until the light comes on, wait a fixed time, then start the shot, to land at the same point in the swing every time.
  5. Brew switch on. The pump and 3-way valve run; pressure builds against the puck, up to the OPV's setting at most.
  6. Brew switch off. The valve vents the group to the drip tray, so the puck is left drier and the portafilter comes off without spraying.
  7. For milk, switch on steam and wait for the heating light to go out, purge the wand, steam, then refill the boiler by running hot water through the wand before the next shot.

Gaggiuino replaces steps 2 to 4 with a temperature that holds still, and step 5 with a pressure or flow profile.
