Silvia Switch LED Mod

Once Gaggiuino runs the brew, steam and hot water switches on low voltage, their lights go dark. This puts them back with an orange LED inside each switch. Read it through once before you open the first one.

Three switches only. The power switch still carries mains and keeps its stock lamp. Leave it alone. Do all of this with the machine unplugged and the switches out of the machine.

Parts, for each of the three switches

  • Orange 5 mm LED
  • Resistor, 1k to 3k ohms, 1/4 W

Tools

  • Thin blade (a utility knife) and a precision screwdriver bit
  • Needle-nose pliers, soldering iron, helping hands
  • Multimeter
  • 5 V phone charger and an old USB cable, cut open
  • Eye protection: springs fly when the switch opens
01

Know the switch

Each switch is two switches side by side (two poles) that flip together. Every pole has a center spade and an outer spade. Stock, the lamp sits across the two center spades, so it lights whenever the switch is on and mains is on the outer spades.

02

Open the switch

  1. Slide a thin blade between the rocker face and the frame on one side and work the first pivot pin out of its hole.
  2. Once that pin is bulging the frame, press the second pin out with the narrower precision bit.
  3. Guide the face out of the frame. Two springs and two levers come loose here; keep them together.
  4. Wedge the white insert out of the face using its four tabs. It's a loose fit.
03

Remove the lamp and studs

Press each stud out from the side the lamp legs are on, using the precision bit. There's no need to cut anything. Leave the studs out for good: the empty holes make threading the LED wires much easier. Keep the lamp and studs in a bag in case you ever go back to stock.

04

Prepare the LED

  1. Hold the LED in helping hands. Solder the resistor to one leg, right up against the resistor body so the rest of that leg stays usable. Either leg works.
  2. Clip the extra off the LED leg and the resistor's far lead, and keep the resistor offcut.
  3. Solder the offcut onto the LED's other leg to lengthen it. Resistor leads are thinner and bend more easily than LED legs.

Note which way round each LED is: the long leg is positive, and the flat edge on the rim marks negative. Build all three the same way and take a photo.

05

Fit the LED into the insert

  1. Lay the LED flat on the amber-window side of the insert.
  2. Thread each lead through a stud hole, or the gap beside it, and wrap it around the pin a spring sits on. Push the wraps down almost flat with the pliers.
  3. Fit the springs back over the pins. They clamp the wires and carry the current from the levers to the LED.
  4. If you're unsure a spring is touching its wire, check with the multimeter now, before closing the face.
06

Put it back together

  1. With the pliers, set each lever on the back of its center spade, round side and raised contact toward the outer spade.
  2. Make sure the springs sit firmly in the back of the face, each resting in the middle valley of its lever.
  3. Fit the face into the frame the right way up: outer spades and amber window both face down. Guide the pivot pins into their holes with the blade.
  4. Nothing should rattle, and the switch should rock without extra force. If it doesn't, open it and reseat the springs and levers.
07

Test it on the bench

Run these before any switch goes near the PCB. Tick them off as you go; the boxes are remembered on this device.

CheckBrewSteamHot water
Off: no continuity between the two outer spades

08

Wire it in

On each switch: 5 V goes to the outer spade of the pole you marked, ground goes to the other outer spade, and the PCB input goes to the center spade on the ground side. Try the switch below to see why that works.

Keep the PCB input on the ground side. If you ever swap 5 V and ground to fix an LED that won't light, move the PCB input wire across with the ground. On the 5 V side, the input would see 5 V every time the switch turns on.

  • 5 V: splice into the 5 V wire of the screen cable with a BN1.25 butt splice, close to the PCB. The Silvia install notes use about 320 mm to the first switch, then 100 mm jumpers between switches.
  • Ground: the same daisy chain to J7 GND that the base install already runs across the three switches.
  • Inputs: the same pins the base install uses; only the spade changes, from wherever it was to the center spade on the ground side.
