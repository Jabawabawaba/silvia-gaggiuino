The machine's metal, the frame, boiler and valve bodies, is bonded to earth for safety. The PCB's 5 V side is deliberately left floating: its ground is not connected to earth anywhere. Everything that crosses between them does so without a wire: the board's sealed power supply module, three optocouplers on the board (for the zero-cross detector, the pump and the 3-way valve) and the SSR's light link. The board's own mains section is live whenever the machine is plugged in.

The reason is the thermocouple. Its tip is screwed into the earthed boiler, and the MAX31855 that reads it must never have its minus input tied to its ground; if it does, it reports a short to ground or reads wrong. An ungrounded thermocouple, with the junction insulated inside the probe, keeps the tip electrically off the boiler, and a floating 5 V side keeps the two from meeting some other way.

So never bond the 5 V ground to earth, and watch for accidental paths: nothing on the low-voltage side should touch the chassis, including a metal screw through one of the board's mounting holes, and a desktop computer on the USB port, which is usually earthed, can make the connection for you. The power-on checks include an open-circuit test between earth and J7 GND.
